# Drafting toward a profile

1. Read the profile for the draft's language, the brief, any narrator or character notes, and nearby accepted text. Where the brief and an approved profile disagree on point of view or tense, ask the author; change neither by inference.
2. Write to the profile: its described voice and its ranges. Dialogue departs from the narration only within the profile's limits for speech.
3. **One take or three.** Regular drafting writes one take. For a final draft, once its plan is settled and the text is meant to be accepted, and for each unit of a restyle, write three independent takes from the same reading: in separate fresh contexts where you can, otherwise each without rereading the others. Three takes cost about three drafts' tokens.
4. Save takes outside the manuscript. Rank several with `rank`; check a single take with `check`.
5. **Read every take and choose one whole.** Record why. Never splice takes. The order is a lead: the reading decides.
6. Check the chosen take with `check` before showing it, and again on its final text. A copy-guard hit blocks it. A measure outside its range is a finding for your review: list the findings first, make one set of fixes, then measure once more and report what is left. Drift that the scene's purpose explains is a question for the author, not a fix.
7. Keep the takes you did not choose, and the ranking, until the unit is accepted or sent back; then delete them. They are never evidence of the author's voice.
