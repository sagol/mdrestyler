## Narration

- Point of view and tense: first person, past tense, written long after the events. The narrator keeps marking the act of remembering and writing, and the gap between then and now.
- Distance and interiority: deep interiority with a double view: the writing self judges, doubts and qualifies the self who lived through it. Events arrive through the narrator's impressions of them; outward action is sparse and is often held back to the end of a long sentence.
- How emotion is handled (named, embodied, deflected): named in abstract nouns and then analyzed; intensity comes through superlatives of feeling and strangeness rather than through the body.
- How thought moves within a paragraph; hesitation, self-correction and mixed feeling: by suspension and revision. The main clause is interrupted by qualifications set off with commas and dashes, by concessions and hedges, and by corrections that restate the idea in a sharper term. A sentence circles its point before it lands.

## Rhythm

- Sentence movement, and how it shifts under pressure or in reflection: long sentences dense with commas, with little swing in length from one sentence to the next; even the short ones are rarely clipped. A shorter sentence lands a revelation after a long approach.
- Paragraphing by scene type: narration in long blocks; dialogue in very short paragraphs, which pulls the median paragraph length down.
- Variation between quiet scenes, argument, intimacy and crisis: crisis slows the prose rather than speeding it; the moment of shock is examined clause by clause.

## Diction

- Register and vocabulary band: educated and polite, Latinate and abstract, with longer words and a wide, varied vocabulary.
- Words and constructions the voice favors (described; quote only own books): many -ly adverbs; nominal phrases built with of; the pluperfect with had; that-clauses; comparisons with as; hedges and concessions (perhaps, doubtless, of course, I think); understatement beside sudden intensifiers.

## Imagery

- Where the images come from; how often figurative language appears: visual detail is sparse and chosen: light, the hour, the shape of a place. Figures of speech are frequent and often extended, drawn from pictures and frames, the stage, royalty, hunting and beasts.

## Dialogue

- How speech is set out and tagged: double quotation marks; tags are often replaced by the narrator's comment on manner or tone.
- How characters are told apart: by register and by how much each knows or will say.
- How a reply answers, evades, interrupts or changes a relationship: speech is elliptical; lines break off at a dash, questions answer questions, and the meaning lies in what is withheld.

## Structure

- Chapter openings and endings; scene breaks; summary against scene: chapters open with reflection on what the previous scene meant; they end on a sharp turn or a single line of speech.

## Tone and humor

- Grave, anxious and self-scrutinizing. Irony is quiet and often at the narrator's own expense. Suspense comes from delay.

## Never

- Author-stated exclusions: none; this profile was built from a reference book, with no author direction. Rare in the source rather than excluded: dialect, slang, short sentences in a row, sentences that open on a conjunction.

## Evidence and limits

- Observations from the scan's samples (the prologue and chapters VI, VII, VIII, XII, XVII) and from a full reading of chapter III; inferences are marked. The profile is built from one short novel, so it describes that narrator's voice, not the author's range.
